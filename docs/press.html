The one-line version

You are a conservator restoring a water-damaged journal written by a woman who spent four years deliberately erasing herself from every record that named her — and the journal was locked so that only her granddaughter could open it. You are not her granddaughter. It works that out.

Why it might be worth your readers' time

There is no hint button, and that is the product. No skip, no nudge, no pity timer — and equally no timers, no limited guesses, no fail states and no lockouts. Every answer field declares the shape of the answer it wants. Everything needed is inside the book.

Almost nothing repeats. Twenty-five distinct solving logics across twenty-eight leaves: a household ledger whose balance is honest and whose prices are not; a radio log where one weekday matters; a census sheet whose own arithmetic forces the line that was scraped away; a poem with one deliberately unanswered rhyme. Each is solved once and never asked again.

The paywall never hides story. All twenty-eight leaves are readable from the first minute, under every instrument the player has calibrated. The purchase gates the act of restoring, not reading — an unusual arrangement that came out of the design's own rules rather than a marketing decision.

One ending is permanent. Where you sign the certificate decides what happens to her. One of the three choices really does delete your restoration. The app asks twice.
