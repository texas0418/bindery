Unwriting — Act I Page Specs

Pages 04 · 06 · 07 · 08 · 09 — the instrument tier bosses and the chains between roots

The five Act I pages that consume keys. Two are the first instrument unlocks (the game's tier bosses); all five must uphold the root pages' law: every solve self-confirms the moment it lands. Chain A (the Calibration Plates) carries the game's deepest taught lesson — trust her marks, not appearances — and its twist foreshadows Act II's reverse chronology. Companion docs: root specs, key graph.

04 Calibration Plate I RAKING LIGHT needs 1961

On the scan

A full-page geometric figure in drafting-precise ink: concentric rings of spokes (12 · 24 · 36 · 48), a rim of 100 fine ticks with every tenth bold, registration crosses in the corners. Penciled beneath: "for the machine, when it comes." The workstation's instrument panel shows RAKING LIGHT — CALIBRATION REQUIRED and opens an alignment interface: the plate can be rotated under the software's modern reticle, which reads out the rim tick currently under zero.

The lock

Geometric alignment (chain A, taught). The plate is drawn deliberately slightly off-center, so aligning by visual symmetry — everyone's first instinct — always reports calibration drift. The rim is a century dial: 100 ticks, 1900–1999. The pencil caption and the accession year (1961, page 01) are the whole key: her zero is the year the journal began.

The turn

Rotate zero to tick 61. The moment it lands, two confirmations fire: a tiny inked dot inside tick 61 — her zero mark, invisible until you look for it — and the software's ring-trace, which now detects the figure's one flaw: the 36-ring has 35 spokes. The lamp calibrates against the gap and RAKING LIGHT unlocks. Taught lesson, stated by the page itself: the figure lies; the marks don't.

Entry format

rotation — rim tick under zero (0–99)

In Vera's voice

"A machine must be told where zero is. I chose my zero long ago."

It reveals

She drew a scanner calibration target decades before scanners existed — the journal was always meant to outlive her and be read by a machine.

06 The Photograph Corners JUNE 1974 needs RAKING

On the scan

An album page: three empty rectangles framed by black photo corners, caption slits cut clean below each. Under normal light, blank paper. Under raking light, the page is a relief map — erased pencil captions survive as embossed impressions, fragmentary where erasure crushed the tooth of the paper.

The lock

Impression reading. The three ghost captions resolve partially: "M——, aged f——" · "the g——h——, our last ——" · "V. & E., J-ne 19-4." The third is a wedding caption with an ambiguous decade digit — 1914 through 1994 all fit the strokes. The disambiguator is physical and on-page (rule 5): one photo corner carries its manufacturer's embossed stamp, KODAVUE — EST. 1968. A corner brand founded in 1968 cannot hold a photograph captioned before it existed.

The turn

The only J-ne 19-4 on or after 1968 is JUNE 1974. On entry, the software raises its raking angle and the third caption's remaining strokes resolve fully — "V. & E., June 1974" — the solve confirming itself in her own recovered hand. First appearance of E., never named.

Entry format

month + four-digit year

In Vera's voice

"I burned the pictures. Paper remembers pressure longer than light."

It reveals

Every photograph was removed by her own hand — and the wedding, June 1974, is the one date she erased most carefully and still could not stop recording.

07 Radio Log NIGHTINGALE needs THURSDAY

On the scan

A ruled log: DATE / TIME / FREQ / NOTES, fourteen rows, all logged between 10 p.m. and 2 a.m. across two months. Taped below, a hand-drawn AM dial arc (530–1600 kHz) — and along the arc, in tiny ink, twenty-six letters seated at irregular frequencies: a homemade cipher wheel, in plain sight (rule 4). Margin, her hand: "our night only. The rest is static."

The lock

Dial mapping. "Our night" is Thursday — meaningless without page 03, the exact ingredient the graph demands. Eleven of the fourteen rows fall on Thursdays; three are decoys whose frequencies decode to noise letters (the punishment for skipping the filter, per the anti-guessing law: wrong path yields garbage, not a near-miss). Each Thursday frequency, read against the taped dial, is a letter.

The turn

Eleven Thursday rows in date order decode to NIGHTINGALE — self-confirming the instant the bird resolves, and doubly so: a night-singing bird, logged only at night. The damage log accepts the word; the NOTES column of the Thursday rows, reread, stops being weather: "clear tonight" / "sang late" / "gone by two."

Entry format

one word, 11 letters

In Vera's voice

"He never touched the radio. Static bored him. So the radio is where I kept what was mine."

It reveals

Thursday was her whole secret architecture — the money under the groceries and a voice on the night air. Nightingale is the one person who still knew she existed.

08 Calibration Plate II ULTRAVIOLET needs JUNE 1974

On the scan

At first glance, Plate I again — same rings, same rim. Recognition is the trap (chain A, twisted). Look closer: the figure is half-drawn, spokes ending in clean stubs mid-air, and the rim now carries two concentric tick rings — 100 fine (years) and 12 coarse (months) — a two-ring combination, like an astrolabe. A small inked arrow rides the outer rim. Pencil caption: "second zero: where we began — and everything ran backward from there." Instrument panel: UV LAMP — TEMPORAL REFERENCE REQUIRED.

The lock

Two-ring alignment keyed to the wedding date (June 1974, page 06). The twist that punishes rote repetition of Plate I's procedure: the outer ring's ticks run counterclockwise — the arrow says so, quietly. A player who aligns by Plate I habit lands on the mirror year (tick 26) and the software reports drift; the doubled lesson is Plate I's lesson applied harder — trust all her marks, including the one that says which way time runs.

The turn

Outer ring to 74 against the arrow, inner ring to 6. The UV lamp floods on, and the plate finishes itself: the missing half of the figure fluoresces exactly where the software's trace predicted the stubs should continue — prediction and revelation matching is the self-confirmation. In the new ink, one fluorescing line: "half of everything I wrote is for you alone." The backward ring is also the game's quietest foreshadow: Act II tells her life in reverse from this exact date.

Entry format

two-ring alignment — year tick + month tick

In Vera's voice

"The first machine found my zero. The second must know when we began — and that everything ran backward from there."

It reveals

Half this book is written in invisible ink — and the marriage is the pivot her whole cipher runs backward from.

09 Margin Arithmetic 14 LARKSPUR needs MAGPIE · GREENHOUSE

On the scan

A long entry about house expenses, its margins crowded with fifteen small addition columns — van hire, deposits, a month of rent, curtains — each column headed by a tiny doodle: some flowers, some birds, a thimble, a kettle. Header, her hand: "what leaving costs." Beneath the last column: "what the magpie keeps buys the door; what the garden grew names the street. Remainders, six-and-twenty, A to Z."

The lock

Modular arithmetic behind a sorting rule that is unreadable without both ingredient words: magpie columns are the bird-headed ones, garden columns the flower-headed ones (pages 02 and 05 are the decoder). The instruction is stated in full — six-and-twenty, A to Z — so the hurdle is visible (rule 4); the work is real: sort fifteen columns, sum eight flower columns' totals mod 26 into letters, sum the bird columns outright. Thimble and kettle columns are inert decoys that poison blind mass-decoding.

The turn

The eight flower columns in page order yield 12 · 1 · 18 · 11 · 19 · 16 · 21 · 18 → L A R K S P U R; the bird columns total 14. The self-confirmation is the best in Act I: larkspur is itself a flower — the garden really did name the street. Answer: 14 LARKSPUR, the house she was saving to flee to.

Entry format

house number + street name

In Vera's voice

"He read every word I ever wrote and never once read the arithmetic. Numbers were beneath him. The house that saved me is spelled in remainders."

It reveals

The margins are her escape budget, priced to the shilling — and 14 Larkspur is where she went, the address page 15 will prove he kept finding anyway.
