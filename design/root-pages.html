Unwriting — Root Page Specs

Pages 01 · 02 · 03 · 05 · 11 · 16 — solvable with nothing; they define the game's contract

The roots are where a player learns what this game considers fair: hard from the first page, no hints ever, every hurdle visible, every answer earnable from what's on the scan. Each spec below is complete — including solutions — because this is the design doc, not the game. Companion to the key dependency graph artifact.

01 Accession 1961

On the scan

Split view: the journal's flyleaf (left) and the Bindery's own intake form for estate lot 44 (right). The flyleaf inscription survives — "For my ———, when she is old enough to ask" — but the name and most of a bookseller's penciled catalog code have been excised by two clean blade strokes. The intake form lists the lot's other eleven items, each with its dealer code and date, and one field flagged red by the software: DATE OF FIRST ENTRY — unverified.

The lock

Catalog cross-reference. The eleven intact dealer codes encode their dates two different plausible ways (day-month-year vs. shelf-month-year, months in Roman numerals) — only one scheme is consistent across all eleven. Applying the proven scheme to the journal's surviving code fragments reconstructs its year field.

The turn

The blade stroke that removed the name also passed through the catalog code — but stopped short of the year. The cut is precise, and a player who notices that precision has met Vera before reading a word of her. Answer: 1961.

Entry format

four-digit year

In Vera's voice

"I left the year. A year alone finds no one."

It reveals

The damage to this book is not damage. It is editing — and the editor knew exactly what information is dangerous.

02 The Dedication MAGPIE

On the scan

The first entry, ruined by bleach bloom — islands of legible text in a white haze, a small ink doodle of a black-and-white bird in the corner. The workstation introduces its OCR confidence layer here via a standard tooltip (diegetic tutorializing, rule 3): each word is shaded by transcription confidence.

The lock

Null cipher. Six words on the page read at 100% confidence while everything around them is degraded. Toggled to confidence view, they stand out like stones in fog — and in context they are grammatically wrong, each slightly misfitting its sentence, because they are not original: they were re-inked years later, over the bleach damage, in the same hand.

The turn

The six re-inked words don't belong to their sentences — they belong to each other. Read in page order, their initials spell MAGPIE; the bird doodle confirms the solve the moment it lands. Two inferences, both discoverable: notice the anomaly (the software shows it), then ask what the anomalous words share.

Entry format

her name for the reader — one word, 6 letters

In Vera's voice

"The bleach was my second edit. What I re-inked, I meant to survive — but only for someone who studies how ink ages."

It reveals

The whole book is addressed to someone she calls Magpie — and Vera came back to this ruined page, years later, to make sure the name would keep.

03 Household Ledger THURSDAY

On the scan

A ruled ledger page in tidy columns — dates, items, amounts, running balance. At the head, in Vera's hand: "March begins, a Sunday." The software's audit note reads balance verified ✓ — and beneath it, smaller: cash out exceeds itemized receipts: 4.85.

The lock

Ledger anomaly. The running balance is arithmetically perfect. The prices are not: several entries charge more for an item than the same item costs elsewhere on the page (eggs at 1.10 against .35 the week before). The falsified entries are the skim — and their dates, computed against "March begins, a Sunday," all land on the same weekday.

The turn

The player audits her the way her husband never did — and discovers the answer is THURSDAY by doing date arithmetic she set up with four words at the top of the page. The puzzle and the story are the same object: this is her escape fund, hidden in plain sight from a man who checked the math and never the groceries.

Entry format

day of the week

In Vera's voice

"He audited arithmetic, never groceries. I kept my sums perfect and my prices dishonest."

It reveals

Every Thursday a little money vanished under the grocery entries — she was saving her escape one falsified egg-price at a time.

05 Pressed Violets GREENHOUSE

On the scan

Ten pressed flowers arranged in a deliberate row on black paper — beautiful, labeled in pencil with species names. Margin note: "mother's stitching, back cover." The inside back cover (browsable — the book is open) holds a pasted-in needlepoint sampler: eight flowers stitched beside eight words, and one stitched aphorism: "a bloom reversed speaks its opposite."

The lock

Floriography with an in-book key (rule 5: nothing outside the game). The sampler maps flowers to words; the arrangement reads as a sentence. Two of the ten flowers are pressed upside down — their meanings invert per the stitched aphorism.

The turn

Decoded with reversals honored, the row reads: remember · the · glass · house · where · we · were · not · found · ever — inverted flowers turning "lost" into "found," "never" into "ever." The sentence's own strange grammar ("not found ever") tells the player the reversals matter. Answer: GREENHOUSE.

Entry format

one word, 10 letters

In Vera's voice

"My mother taught me flowers before the county taught me forms. It is the one language he never learned."

It reveals

The flowers come from the garden where she and her daughter were happiest; the garden no longer exists, and "not found ever" was the point of it.

11 Carbon Paper R → slot 13

On the scan

A sheet of used carbon paper, bound in, near-black and shining. The workstation's standard invert and mirror toggles sit in the toolbar (ordinary scan tools — no instrument needed). Mirrored and inverted, the sheet holds typed text — doubled, two documents struck through the same carbon, interleaved.

The lock

Mirror reconstruction plus disentanglement. One document is her formal letter to the county requesting her own records; underneath it, offset by a line, her earlier draft of the same letter — one paragraph different. Where the fair copy says "for personal reference," the draft says what she meant: "to learn what he can see."

The turn

At the reconstructed letter's foot, the county's received-stamp shows a reference the typist recorded back onto the carbon — REF 13-R — and in white pencil, visible only inverted, Vera's annotation: "the last letter is the one they kept." The damage log records R → slot 13.

Entry format

restoration key — one letter + slot number

In Vera's voice

"He burned letters. Nobody burns the carbon."

It reveals

She asked the county for her own records and was refused — "requester could not be verified" — the machinery of documentation no longer believed she existed.

16 Library Slip S → slot 6

On the scan

A borrower's pocket and date-due slip: fourteen due-date stamps spanning 1968–1985, and a penciled column of call numbers with faint titles — privacy statutes, bookbinding, the chemistry of ink. The software's cadence note: non-standard renewal pattern detected.

The lock

Date-interval cipher. The gaps between consecutive stamps, in days, map to the alphabet (1–26). Fourteen stamps yield thirteen intervals; the first eight decode, the rest are noise from genuine renewals — and telling signal from noise is part of the solve (the signal intervals never exceed 26).

The turn

The eight signal intervals — 19 · 9 · 24 · 20 · 8 · 9 · 19 · 19 — decode to S I X T H I S S: "SIXTH IS S." The message is self-confirming the instant it resolves into words, which is what makes a brutal mechanism fair. The damage log records S → slot 6.

Entry format

restoration key — one letter + slot number

In Vera's voice

"Ruth never asked why I renewed on odd days. She stamped. The stamps remembered."

It reveals

Seventeen years of borrowing books on how to disappear — and a librarian who never once reported her overdue.

Doctrine notes: all six hurdles are visible on arrival (rule 4 — a red field, a confidence toggle, an audit discrepancy, a strange grammar, a toolbar, a cadence note); all keys are in-book (rule 5 — the floriography key is the sampler, the weekday anchor is written at the ledger's head); every solve is self-confirming at the moment it lands, which is the no-hints game's substitute for validation. Letter-slot assignments made here: R→13, S→6 (R was first assigned slot 12 — but slot 12 of VERAOSTRANDER is E; caught by the repo's graph test); remaining eleven assigned during Act II page design. Difficulty order for onboarding-by-browsing: 03 and 05 crack first for most players, 16 last.
