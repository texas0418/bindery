01 Accession 1961

The trail

  1. The flyleaf's catalog code fragment reads ▮—VI–61 — the shelf letter is cut away; a Roman numeral and two digits survive.
  2. The intake form's six lot items each carry a code shaped letter–roman–digits. Test the hypothesis against the descriptions: Ledger "final entry NOV 1951" → A–XI–51: XI = November, 51 = 1951. Every item agrees: roman = month, digits = year.
  3. The deliberate decoy: the Atlas is "printed MAR 1948, plate 44" with code D–III–48. If digits meant plate numbers, it would end 44. It ends 48 — the year reading is the only scheme that fits all six (the software's note says as much).
  4. Apply to the fragment: VI = June, 61 = 1961. The margin note ("the cut stopped short of the digits") is the story: she left the year on purpose.

After: page ✓, restored passage appears, DAMAGE LOG shows 1961 · p01. Bench: Calibration Plate I flips from "needs key · p01" to attackable; ribbon moves.

04 Calibration Plate I 61 → RAKING LIGHT

The trail

  1. The instrument panel wants a rim tick (0–99) and says the rim "spans a century" — 100 ticks = the years 1900–1999.
  2. The figure is drawn slightly off-center, so eyeballing symmetry is a dead end by design.
  3. The description hides the confirmation: "inside one fine tick — one past the sixth bold — a dot no wider than a needle." Sixth bold = 60; one past = tick 61.
  4. 61 = 1961, the accession year from page 01 — her zero is the year the journal began. Enter 61.

05 Pressed Violets GREENHOUSE

The trail

  1. Ten pressed blooms in a row, two of them crown-down (flax at 3, thistle at 8). The margin points to the sampler: each bloom stitched twice, crown-up and crown-down, a word to each crown — and the aphorism "a bloom reversed speaks its opposite crown."
  2. Reading everything upright gives a complete, warm sentence: remember the STONE house where we were FOUND so gladly — and STONEHOUSE fits the ten-letter format perfectly. It fails. That rejection is the aphorism collecting its due (the trap punishes skipping the orientation column).
  3. Honor the two overturned blooms — their crown-down words: stone→glass, found→lost: remember the GLASS house where we were LOST so gladly.
  4. The glass house, one word, 10 letters: GREENHOUSE. The reversal decides which house the row remembers — nothing decodes without it.

After: log shows GREENHOUSE · p05; Margin Arithmetic (09) unlocks (with MAGPIE); First Day (22) stays SCAN QUEUED.
