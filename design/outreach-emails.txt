TITLE:
I spent a month building a puzzle journal with no hint button, and I'd like
to know if that was a mistake

BODY:
The Bindery: Unwriting is 28 hand-authored leaves in the Journal 29 and Rusty
Lake tradition. You play a conservator restoring the journal of a woman who
deliberately erased herself from every public record to escape a controlling
husband, and who locked the book so only her granddaughter could read it.

The design decision I keep going back and forth on: no hints at all. No skip,
no nudge, no ladder. To make that fair rather than cruel I gave up the usual
pressure valves too - no timers, no limited guesses, no fail states, and every
answer field tells you the shape of the answer it wants. The bar I set myself
was that a stuck player shown the answer should say "I had everything I
needed," not "how was I supposed to know that."

Twenty-five of the twenty-eight leaves use a mechanism that appears nowhere
else in the book. My favourite is a census sheet where the enumerator's own
arithmetic forces the identity of the line that was scraped off the page.

Act I is free if you want to see whether it is your kind of thing.
https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6797783492

Genuinely interested in the no-hints argument if you think it is the wrong
call - it is the part I am least sure about.
