SUBJECT: The Bindery: Unwriting - iOS puzzle journal, no hints, out now

Hi,

I've just put out The Bindery: Unwriting on iOS. It is a puzzle journal in
the Journal 29 mould, with one difference that will either interest you or
put you straight off: it has no hint system at all.

You play a conservator restoring a water-damaged journal. The woman who
wrote it was an archivist who spent four years taking herself out of every
record that named her, to get away from a husband who used paperwork the way
other men use their hands. She locked the book so that only her
granddaughter could open it. You are not her granddaughter, and partway
through, the journal works that out.

There are twenty-eight leaves and twenty-five of them use a solving method
that appears nowhere else in the book. One is a census sheet where the
enumerator's own arithmetic forces the identity of the line that was scraped
off the page.

Cutting hints meant cutting the punishments too. Nothing is timed, nothing
runs out, and there is no way to fail a leaf. Every answer field tells you
the shape of the answer it wants.

The part that matters for a review: the purchase gates restoring a leaf, not
reading one. All twenty-eight are browsable from the first minute. Act I
restores free and the rest is a single 5.99 unlock.

TITLE:
I spent a month building a puzzle journal with no hint button, and I'd like
to know if that was a mistake

BODY:
The Bindery: Unwriting is 28 hand-authored leaves in the Journal 29 and Rusty
Lake tradition. You play a conservator restoring the journal of a woman who
deliberately erased herself from every public record to escape a controlling
husband, and who locked the book so only her granddaughter could read it.

The design decision I keep going back and forth on: no hints at all. No skip,
no nudge, no ladder. To make that fair rather than cruel I gave up the usual
pressure valves too - no timers, no limited guesses, no fail states, and every
answer field tells you the shape of the answer it wants. The bar I set myself
was that a stuck player shown the answer should say "I had everything I
needed," not "how was I supposed to know that."

Twenty-five of the twenty-eight leaves use a mechanism that appears nowhere
else in the book. My favourite is a census sheet where the enumerator's own
arithmetic forces the identity of the line that was scraped off the page.

Act I is free if you want to see whether it is your kind of thing.
https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6797783492

Genuinely interested in the no-hints argument if you think it is the wrong
call - it is the part I am least sure about.
